## Eutrophication
**Major pollutants**: Nitrate and phosphate

**Decomposition**: 
C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> + 6O<sub>2</sub> -> 6CO<sub>2</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O
N -> NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> / NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>
P -> PO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>
### *Impact*
1. **Increase turbidity**
2. **Rate of deposition of sediment increases**: increased vegetation cover reducing the speed of water flow
3. **Increased net primary productivity**
4. **Accumulation of organic material and decreased concentration of dissolved oxygen**
5. **Decreased species diversity of producers**: Cyanobacteria becomes the dominant species
6. **Decreased fish diversity**

### *The effect of eutrophication on ecosystem services*
1. **Effect on fishing**
	1. fish dies or migrates away
	2. leads to dominant species such as carp, stunted panfish
2. **Effect on recreation**
	1. Reduces the potential for fishing, sailing, paddle-boarding and swimming
	2. the presence and smell of scum may make waterbodies **unpleasant to view and smell**
3. **Aesthetics**
	1. Reducing the visual appeal
	2. Unpleasant smell
4. **Health concerns**
	1. Excess nitrates will increase rates of stomach cancer or blue baby syndrome in infants

### *Feedbacks*
**Positive**
1. DOM increase -> More frequent docomposition -> O<sub>2</sub> decreases-> fishes decrease -> DOM increase
2. Algae increase -> Sunlight penetrated through water decrease -> Underwater plantish decrease -> DOM increase -> decomposition will release more nutrients -> Algae increase

**Negative**
1. DOM increase -> Decomposition consumes O<sub>2</sub> -> O<sub>2</sub> decrease ->fish decrease -> DOM increase-> Concentration of Nitrate and phosphate increase -> algae increase -> concentration of nutrients decrease
2. DOM increase -> Decomposition increase -> DOM decrease
