#### 1. Fisheries
- ##### **Unsustainable Fishing Methods**:
	1. **Bottom Trawling**: Large net dragged behind a boat
		- **Issues**: Bycatch; Disturbance of sediment; Habitat destruction
	2. **Ghost fishing**: Abandoned nets, constantly catching fish
		- **Issues**: Ghost gear continuously catching and killing fish and other animals
	3. **Cyanide fishing**: Low dose of sodium cyanide to stun fish and catch alive
		- **Issues**: Can kill other non-target species; ecosystem destruction; attractive since live fish have higher price
	4. **Blast fishing**: Explosion
		- **Issues**: Dangerous; habitat destruction; non-target; many fish killed and not caught
- ##### **Unsustainable Fishing Industry**
	- Satellite technology, GPS
	- **Blast freezing**: Freeze the fish immediately, allow the fishing fleet to continuously fishing for months
	- Fleet become bigger and bigger
- ##### **Sustainability of fisheries**
	- **Sustainability yield(SY)** - Def
	-  **Annual sustainable yield**
		- SY=total biomass at time **t+1** - total biomass at time **t** 
		- SY=annual **birth** and **recruitment** - annual **death** and **emigration**
	- MSY=SY=annual sustainable yield
- ##### **Mitigation Strategy**
	- **Changing the fishing industry**
		- Population of fish must be monitored to assess whether they are in danger
		- Closed seasons can be put in place to reduce fishing during the breeding seasons
		- Nets sizes of fish must be monitored and controlled
		- Laws enforcement - Protection area - **MPA(Marine Protection Area)**
		- Impose total allowable catches
		- Fishing license can be issued
		- boat capacity management into an area
		- new satellite tracking systems are being used to help fight against private fishing
	- **International and national policy**
		- ICES
			- Monitors harvests in the North Atlantic
		- IUCN
			- Restricting the sizes of nets
			- Ban on drifting nets
			- Imposing quotas for different fish
			- Creating exclusion zones(MPA)
	- **Changing consumer behavior**
		- Local and individual action
			- "Check the fish on your dish" campaign
			- MSC(Marine steward ship council)
#### 2. Aquaculture
- **Problem**
	- Loss of **habitat** of native species when cages area placed
	- Disruption of **natural food chains and webs**
		- e.g. herring and anchovy as fish feeds for salmon
	- **Spread of disease**
	- Escape of species from the farms may affect local **gene pool** - **Outcompete**
	- **Pollution** of local environment: Fish feed, antibiotics, fish feces->**Eutrophication**

**3. Collapse and recovery of Peruvian anchovy**
- Reasons for collapse
	- Population decrease (overfishing)
	- Political concerns (common fishing)
	- Climate variability - cold temperature
- Strategies for recovery
	- total allowable catches
	- scientific monitoring
	- regional agreement between UK, Sweden, Norway
**3. Collapse and recovery of Peruvian anchovy**
- Reasons for collapse
	- unsustainable fishing
	- EL Nino (climate change) - 厄尔尼诺现象
	- Poor management
- Strategies for recovery
	- natural recovery
	- Individual vessel quotas(IUQs)
	- seasonal & spatial closure
	- IMARPE
**4. The environmental impact of shrimp farming in Ecuador**
- Mangrove loss: cut for land, decreased 70%
- Biodiversity loss: mangrove provides habitat (e.g. crab birds)
- Water pollution - fertilizer
- land salinization
**5. The environmental impact of Norwegian salmon fish farming**
- Escaped salmon
	- genetic dilution - lower resistance to wild environment - may pass their DNA to wild type
	- outcompete
- Pollution
	- organic waste: 1. food 2. feces -> sludge carpet
	- Inorganic waste: pesticides
		- non-target effect
		- resistance evolution
		- fish stress
- Disease: higher density -> more easy to infect -> spread to wild population
**6. The benefits of rice-fish farming**
1. More environmentally friendly due to reduced use of pesticides and fish feeds, improve water quality
2. Fish will lower rice disease incidence and pest damage, increase the productivity of rice
3. Fish waste increased ​soil organic matte, improving microbial activity and reducing erosion risks 
4. Rice-fish systems preserved aquatic biodiversity (e.g., native shrimp, frogs) and acted as artificial wetlands, boosting ecological resilience in degraded landscapes.
