### Population System
1. **input**
	1. immigration
	2. birth
2. **output**
	1. emmigration
	2. death
3. **Indicators to quantify population**
	1. **==CBR - Crude Birth Rate (‰):** the number of live births per 1000 people in a population per year==
		1. Total number of births / Total population * 1000 (‰)
		2. Strength
			1. Can be used to calculate NIR which indicates the overall trend of population
		3. Weakness
			1. Not cover immigration
	2. ==**CDR - Crude Death Rate (‰):** the number of deaths per 1000 people in a population per year==
		1. Total number of deaths / Total population * 1000 (‰)
		2.  Strength
			1. Can be used to calculate NIR which indicates the overall trend of population
			2. Not cover emmigration
	3. **==NIR - Natural Increase Rate (%):** the difference between the CBR and CDR, expressed as a percentage==
		1. (CBR-CDR) / 10
	4. **==DT - Doubling Time:** the length of time it takes for a population to double in size, assuming its natural increase rate remains constant==
		1. 70 / **NIR (%)** -> number in front of %
	5. ==**TFR - Total Fertility Rate:** the average number of birth a women has in her lifetime==
		1. 2.1 -> **replacement rate**
		2. Strength
			1. Can use replacement to examine the population trend
	6. ==**FR - Fertility Rate:** the average number of births per thousand women of child-bearing age (15-45)==

### Models to predict population growth
1. **Age-sex pyramid**
	1. Wide/narrow base: birth rate
	2. Wide/narrow top: death rate, life span
	3. Indents: emigrants(workers)
	4. bulges: imigrants, baby boom
	5. concave/vertical slope: death rate
2. **Demographic transition model (DTM)**
	1. stage 1: high stationary (pre-industrial)
		1. high birth rate
		2. high death rate
		3. stable population
		4. e.g. indigenous tribes
	2. stage 2: early expanding (LEDC)
		1. high birth rate
		2. dropped death rate
		3. growing population
		4. e.g. Sudan, Afghanistan
	3. stage 3: late expanding (wealthier LEDC)
	4. stage 4: low stationary (MEDC)
	5. stage 5: declinging (MEDC)
