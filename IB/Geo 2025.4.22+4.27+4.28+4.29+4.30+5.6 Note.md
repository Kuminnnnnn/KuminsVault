# **Glacial Environment**
## Glacier system
> the relationship between **total accumulation(input)** and **total ablation(output)**
### Glacier advance
1. Net accumulation of snow - in the **zone of accumulation**
2. Net melting - **Ablation** - in the **zone of ablation**
	- due to low **altitude** 
	- low **temp** - melting > accumulation
3. Melting and iceberg calving
- May be caused by **global dimming**
### Glacier retreat
- May  be caused by **global warming or greenhouse effect**

## **Glacial Transportation**
- **Supraglacial debris**: on glacial surface
	- will become englacial debris due to accumulation
- **Englacial debris**: inside of glacier
- **Subglacial debris**

## **Glacial Erosion**
### Process
- ==**Abrasion**: the debris carried by the glacier scrapes and scratches the rock==
- ==**Plucking**: Mostly effective in jointed rocks or those weakened by freeze-thaw. As the ice moves, meltwater seeps into the joints and freezes to the rock, which is then ripped out by the moving==
### Landscape
#### Group 1:
- **Cirque/Corrie**
	- Formation:
		1. a **preglacial hollow** is **enlarged** by freeze-thaw and removal of snowmelt(nivation)
		2. **ice accumulate** in the hollow
		3. ice reached a **critical weight and depth**, the ice moves out in a ==***rotational manner***== eroding the floor by **plucking** and **abrasion**
		4. Repeating
		5. Melt water in the cirque forming a lake name **Tarn**
- **Arete**: is the sharp-edge ridge formed when tow cirques erode back towards each other
- **Pyramidal Peak**
#### Group 2:
- **Trough/u-shaped valley**
	- 1. Glaciers cut distinctive U-shaped valleys with a flat floor and steep sides
	- 2. The glaciers widens, steepens and smoothens V-shaped river valleys
	- **Ribbon lake** usually found in a glacial trough
- **Hanging valley**：are formed by tributary glaciers which do not cut down to the level of the main valley but are left suspended above

#### Group 3:
- **Roche moutonnee**/**Sheepback**: formed by the passing of glacier
	- Create asymmetric erosional landform
	- Upstream side: abrasion
	- Downstream side: plucking

## **Glacial Deposition**
- Can be seen when glaciers retreat or their abilities of transporting materials decreases
- Angular and unsorted glacial deposits are called **till**, which inclued:
	- **Erratics**: glacier-tranported rock fragment that differ from the local bedrock
	- **Moraines**: are loose rocks, weathered from the valley sides and carried by glaciers
		- **Lateral moraine**: weathered from mountains
		- **Medial moraine**: when two glacier met, lateral moraine met
		- **Terminal moraine**: the furthest distance the glacier has reached, moraine left up
		- (**Snout**: the leading head of the glacier)
	- **Drumlins**: oval or elongated hill believed to have been formed by the streamlined movement of glacial ice sheets across rock debris, or till
		- Mostly appear in **Continental Glacier Area**
- Glacial and fluvioglacial deposits left after the ice has melted are called **drift**

# **Periglacial Environment**
## **Permafrost**
Permanently frozen ground
Including:
1. Continuous
2. Discontinuous
3. Sporadic
**Talik**: an unfrozen ground surrounded by permafrost
	1. open talik
	2. closed talik
	3. through talik

## **Periglacial processes**
- **Weathering Process**
	- Frost-shathering
- **Mass movement**: the movement of surface material caused by gravity
	- Solifluction
	- Frost creep

## **Periglacial landform**
- **Pattern ground**: is the distinct, and often symmetrical natural pattern of geometric shapes formed by ground material in periglacial
- **Frost Heave**
- **Ice Wedge Formation**: Caused by ground contraction
