## Climate and weather
> **Climate**: describes how the atmosphere behaves over relatively long periods of time
> **Weather**:  describes the condition in the atmosphere over a short period of time

**Difference**: time scale
**Similarity**: both are affected by ocean and atmospheric circulatory systems
### Climate change debate
**Human activity is responsible**
1. Evidence from sediments/ice cores of human induced warming
2. current rate of warming is unprecedented
3. increase in GHG from human activities correlates with increase in global temperature
4. evidence of dramatic effects of global warming in recent times
5. positive feedback loops

**Human activity is not responsible**
1. Part of natural cycles are not human induced
2. technology will provide solutions to any problems
3. prediction models of climate change are inaccurate
4. impacts are over exaggerated
5. global warming may be mitigated via negative feedback mechanisms
6. scientists are self serving and manipulate results to produce findings that attract funding for further research

## Evidence of Climate Change
1. **Anthropogenic CO<sub>2</sub> emissions**
	1. Preindustrial era: 280ppm
	2. late 1950s: 315ppm
	3. 2020s: 420ppm
2. **Ice cores**
	1. tiny bubbles from ancient atmosphere
3. **Tree rings**
	1. wide rings: warm, wet
	2. narrower rings: drought, cold
4. **Sediments**
	1. pollen from oak and cedar found in Sahara -> once more moist
	2. former lake terraces -> once more moist
	3. fossil sand dunes -> once drier
	4. carbon and oxygen isotopes in the shells of microorganisms of ocean floor

## Stratospheric ozone (good ozone)
25-30km
### Formation
1. O<sub>2</sub> decompose by UV -> O + O
2. O + O<sub>2</sub> -> O<sub>3</sub> 
### Break down
1. O + O<sub>3</sub> -UV-> 2O2
2. 2O<sub>3</sub> = 3O<sub>2</sub> 
### The role of good ozone
1. Absorbs incoming UV radiation from sunlight
2. Absorbs some out-going long-wave radiation -> reduce GHE

## The Montreal Protocol
- Phase out the production of ozone-depleting substance
- **Strenth**:
	- signed uo to by most states (197)
	- inclusive of developing nations as protocol provides support for implementation
	- implementation of legislation has been very successful in reducing emissions of ODS
	- some replacements for ODS have been found
	- Ozone hole is shrinking
	- first universally agreed protocol
	- ozone levels are expected to recover in the future
- **Limitations**:
	- not all nations are initially involved with the protocol
	- illegal trade still exists(a black market trade for banned ODS continued)
	- alternatives of some ODS are not yet available thus are not exempt from protocol
	- Some replacements for ODS are also ozone depleting(e.g. HCFC)
	- Some replacements for ODS are GHGs (e.g. HFC)
	- Not suitable disposal facilities for old ODS (ODS has very long life spans)
	- The effect of the Montreal Protocol will take a long time to occur
