## Natural resources

- water

## Natural capital
> the stock of natural resources available on earth

1. **Natural resources that have economic value**
	1. trees
	2. soil
	3. water
	4. living organisms
	5. ores
2. **Natural resources that provide life-supporting services**
	1. forest - food and erosion protection
	2. photosynthesis
	3. water cycle
___
## Natural income
> the yield obtained from natural resources, including goods and services

**Natural resources** refers to *all available resources on earth*, those *are beneficial to human* are **natural capital**, which will provide **natural income**
___
## Different Point of View
- **Ecocentric**
	- any **over-exploitation** will lead to self demise
	- case: Dobe Kung indigenous tribe - hunter gatherer
- **Technocentric**
	- resources are there to be when shortage occurs
	- there will always be new ideas to make more resources or new resources
	- case: Green Revolution in farming; GM crops
- **Anthropocentric**
	- rules and regulations that may allow for increased exploitation
	- agriculture and fishing policies -> govern the exploitation rate

- **Pros for the concept of natural capital and natural income**
	1. lead to **more sustainable resource management**
		1. sustainable forestry
		2. fishing practices
- **Cons**
	1. overlook the **intrinsic value**
	2. seeing nature as **commodities** for sale rather than a **public good that should be protected** for benefits of all
___
## Recyclable resources
recyclable resources can be used over and over, but must **first go through a process** to prepare it for reuse

## I. **Non-renewable Energy**

### 🪨 **Coal**

| Category      | Notes                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Facts         | • Fossilised plants from the Carboniferous period  <br>• Mined by open-cast or underground methods  <br>• Burned to heat water → steam drives turbines                      |
| Advantages    | • Plentiful supply (≈250 years left)  <br>• Easy to transport (solid)  <br>• Cheap and well-developed technology                                                            |
| Disadvantages | • Non-renewable  <br>• Releases large amounts of CO₂  <br>• Sulphur → SO₂ → acid deposition  <br>• Air pollution (soot, lung disease)  <br>• Mining causes land degradation |

---

### 🛢️ **Oil**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Fossilised microorganisms  <br>• Found in porous rocks  <br>• Refined by fractional distillation|
|Advantages|• High heat of combustion  <br>• Many uses (transport, industry)  <br>• Relatively cheap once discovered|
|Disadvantages|• Limited supply (≈20–50 years)  <br>• CO₂ emissions  <br>• Oil spill risks  <br>• Pipelines vulnerable to attack|

---

### 🔥 **Natural Gas**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Mainly methane  <br>• Often found with oil  <br>• Used for heating and electricity|
|Advantages|• Highest heat of combustion  <br>• Cleaner than coal and oil  <br>• Relatively cheap|
|Disadvantages|• Non-renewable (≈70 years)  <br>• Still releases CO₂|

---

### ☢️ **Nuclear Fission**

| Category      | Notes                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Facts         | • Uranium nuclei split to release energy  <br>• Australia has large reserves  <br>• ≈80 years of fuel at current rates                                     |
| Advantages    | • Very high energy output per unit mass  <br>• No CO₂ during operation                                                                                     |
| Disadvantages | • Very high construction costs  <br>• Radioactive waste (thousands of years)  <br>• Accident risk (e.g. Chernobyl)  <br>• Nuclear weapons / terrorism risk |

---

## II. **Renewable Energy**

### 💧 **Hydroelectric Power (HEP)**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Moving water turns turbines  <br>• Can include pumped-storage|
|Advantages|• Reliable, high-quality energy  <br>• Low operating emissions  <br>• Reservoirs provide water & recreation|
|Disadvantages|• Expensive to build  <br>• Floods land and communities  <br>• Disrupts river ecosystems  <br>• Sediment trapping affects deltas|

---

### 🌿 **Biomass**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Organic waste or crops burned or fermented  <br>• Can produce biofuels|
|Advantages|• Cheap and accessible  <br>• Renewable if replanted|
|Disadvantages|• Competes with food crops  <br>• Emits greenhouse gases when burned  <br>• Unsustainable if not replanted|

---

### 🌳 **Wood**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Trees felled or coppiced  <br>• Burned for heat and light|
|Advantages|• Cheap and widely available  <br>• Renewable if trees replanted|
|Disadvantages|• Low energy density  <br>• Air pollution  <br>• Transport costs high  <br>• Deforestation if unmanaged|

---

### ☀️ **Solar – Photovoltaic (PV)**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Converts sunlight directly into electricity|
|Advantages|• Potentially infinite supply  <br>• Safe and clean  <br>• Suitable for small-scale use|
|Disadvantages|• High initial cost  <br>• Intermittent (no sun at night)  <br>• Needs regular cleaning|

---

### ☀️ **Concentrated Solar Power (CSP)**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Mirrors focus sunlight to create heat  <br>• Steam drives turbines|
|Advantages|• Renewable  <br>• Large-scale electricity generation|
|Disadvantages|• Needs high solar radiation  <br>• Limited locations  <br>• Technology still developing|

---

### 🏠 **Solar – Passive**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Building design captures and stores heat|
|Advantages|• Very low running cost  <br>• No emissions|
|Disadvantages|• Requires specialised building design|

---

### 🌬️ **Wind**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Wind turbines generate electricity  <br>• Often grouped in wind farms|
|Advantages|• Clean and renewable  <br>• Low operating costs|
|Disadvantages|• Intermittent supply  <br>• Visual and noise pollution  <br>• Bird migration concerns  <br>• High installation cost|

---

### 🌊 **Tidal**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Tidal movement drives turbines  <br>• Barrages built across estuaries|
|Advantages|• Very predictable  <br>• High energy potential|
|Disadvantages|• Extremely costly  <br>• Few suitable sites  <br>• Disrupts ecosystems and shipping|

---

### 🌊 **Wave**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Wave motion drives turbines via air or water|
|Advantages|• Suitable for island nations  <br>• Local-scale generation|
|Disadvantages|• Expensive  <br>• Storm damage risk  <br>• Environmental objections|

---

### 🌋 **Geothermal**

|Category|Notes|
|---|---|
|Facts|• Uses heat from Earth’s interior  <br>• Best in volcanic regions|
|Advantages|• Reliable, constant supply  <br>• Low emissions|
|Disadvantages|• High setup cost  <br>• Limited locations  <br>• Risk from underground gases|
