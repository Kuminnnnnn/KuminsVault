___
## Different Point of View
- **Ecocentric**
	- any **over-exploitation** will lead to self demise
	- case: Dobe Kung indigenous tribe - hunter gatherer
- **Technocentric**
	- resources are there to be when shortage occurs
	- there will always be new ideas to make more resources or new resources
	- case: Green Revolution in farming; GM crops
- **Anthropocentric**
	- rules and regulations that may allow for increased exploitation
	- agriculture and fishing policies -> govern the exploitation rate

- **Pros for the concept of natural capital and natural income**
	1. lead to **more sustainable resource management**
		1. sustainable forestry
		2. fishing practices
- **Cons**
	1. overlook the **intrinsic value**
	2. seeing nature as **commodities** for sale rather than a **public good that should be protected** for benefits of all

## The value of natural capital
1. **Use value**
	1. **economic value** - price of markable goods
	2. **technological value** - providing raw material
		1. e.g. Uranium, Lithium
	3. **ecological value** - eco functions
	4. **recreational value** - natural park
2. **Non-use value**

## Example of changing value of natural captial
1. **Oil**
	1. Middle age - treat wounds
	2. 20th and 21st centuries
		1. fuels
		2. industrial raw materials for plastic and chemical fertilizer
	3. 21st centuries
		1. global warming -> shift interest into renewable energy source
2. **Uranium**
	1. Before mid 20th - no value
	2. 20th century -> valuable due to the development of nuclear power
	3. shift from acceptance into reluctance due historical events - Fukushima nuclear disaster
3. **Lithium**
	1. 30yrs ago - not valuable
	2. now - batteries
	3. future - alternative sources
4. **Cork**
	1. for many years - bottle stoppers -> highly valued in place where grapes are grown for wine
	2. last 20 years - plastic lids replaced
	3. future: value may increase again due to plastic bans
5. **Whale oil**
	1. past: lightning
	2. now: electricity

## Non-biodegradable waste and pollution
- **biodegradable**: capable of being broken down by natural biological processes
- **pollution**: the introduction of harmful substances into the environment at a faster rate than they can be removed or converted into harmless substances
