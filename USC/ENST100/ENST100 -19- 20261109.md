# Waste Generation and Disposal
## Municipal Solid Waste
- Common household waste plus office and retail wastes, excluding industrial, hazardous, and construction wastes.
- Why so much?
	- items for convenience
	- single use
	- changing fashion
	- low quality consumer goods
	- updating to newest technology
---
## 5 ways to get rid of waste
- **Landfill**
	- most common
	- require sanitation
	- Environmental **Impacts**
		- methane and other GHG
		- Leachate -> groundwater
			- ammonia, toxic substances..
		- Destroying natural habitat
		- Neighborhood impacts
			- trucks driving through
			- air pollution
			- smell
	- **Siting**
		- EPA rules
			- groundwater monitoring
				- check for pollutants semi-annually
				- If one is too high monitor more closely and apply corrective action
			- Closure and post-closure care requirements
				- Plan and timeline for closure
				- Maintain integrity of leachate collection, groundwater monitoring, methane monitoring, and final cover
			- Financial assurance
				- Must demonstrate ability to pay for closure and post-closure care
		- ![[Siting.png]]
- **Incineration**
	- combustion to convert to energy
- **Gasification**
- **Recycling**
	- Two Types
		- Closed loop: recycling it into the same product
		- Open loop: into another product
	- Loss occurs at many stages (especially waste collection)
	- Plastics are not infinitely recyclable  
		- Quality decreases with time (polymers get shorter, impurities difficult to separate)  
		- You can make different things (fleece from milk jugs) or recycle only 2-3 times  
		- Most plastics ever made are discarded (landfill or environment) 
	- About 10% of plastics are recycled
- **Composting**
	- organic material break down by microorganisms
	- compost is a biologically-stable soil amendment produced by the aerobic decomposition or organic material
	- ~63% of MSW is compostable
	- *California Senate Bill 1383*
	- Scale
		- Home composting
		- Community composting
		- Municipal composting

## E-waste
- Small component of waste by weight and volume
- Disproportionate amount of toxic substances
	- heavy metals like lead, mercury and cadmium
- little e-waste is recycled
