## Pollutants
1. **Sulfur Dioxide**
	1. colorless, odorless gas
	2. converted through oxidation reactions into fine particulate sulfate
	3. Souces
		1. burning of coals
		2. industrial processes
		3. Volcanoes, fires, geothermal springs
	4. adverse effects
			1. corrosion of paint and metals
			2. injury of plants or animals
			3. severe damage to lungs
			4. acid rain
2. **NO<sub>x</sub>**
	1. emitted largely as NO or NO2
	2. visible as smog
	3. Souces
		1. automobiles
		2. fossil fuel plants
		3. agriculture
		4. lightning, volcanoes, fires, microbes
	4. adverse effects
		1. smog, ozone, acid rain
		2. irritation to eyes, throats and lungs
		3. more susceptibility to diseases
	5. Pollution control
		1. Selective Catalytic Reduction
3. **CO**
	1. colorless
	2. odorless
	3. Souces
		1. automobiles
		2. indoor gas appliances
		3. volcanoes and fires
	4. Adverse effects
		1. extremely toxic to human and animals
		2. Hazardous to people with heart disease, anemia, respiratory disease
		3. May cause birth defects 
		4. Worse at higher altitudes
4. **Ozone**
	1. colorless gas
	2. slightly sweet odor
	3. difficult to regulate. 
	4. Pollutant whose health standard is most frequently exceeded in urban areas (L.A. #1 offender!) 
	5. Source: 
		1. Produced on bright sunny days 
	6. Adverse effects (dose dependent): 
		1. Damage to rubber, paint, textiles  
		2. Reduces plant growth; causes death at high concentrations 
		3. Humans and animals: damage to eyes, respiratory system
	7. Good and Bad Ozone
		1. Troposphere 0-11km -> Bad
		2. Stratosphere 11-50km -> Good
			1. Block UVB and UVC
		3. Thermosphere >100-600km -> Good
			1. Block UV and Xray
	8. Depletion of good ozone
		1. CFCs
5. **Particulate**
	1. Particles suspended in the air < than 10 μm and < 2.5 μm.  
	2. Very fine particles (<2.5 μm) easily inhaled into lungs where they are  
	3. absorbed into bloodstream. Exhaust, coal ash, etc.  
	4. Sources: 
		1. farming, roads, industrial processes  
		2. Natural sources: windstorms, volcanoes, fires  
	5. Adverse effects:  
		1. U.S. 60,000 deaths per year  
		2. Linked to lung cancer and bronchitis  
		3. Particularly bad in urban areas  
		4. Causes global dimming (counteracts process of – global warming by cooling the atmosphere)
6. **Lead**
	1. Heavy metal
	2. spread globally through air particles (found in greenland ice sheet)  
	3. Sources: 
		1. Automobile batteries and other industrial products, previously used as a gasoline additive  
	4. Adverse effects: 
		1. neurological, learning/brain development  
		2. Enters terrestrial and aquatic food chains; toxic effects on organisms
