# Nonrenewable Energy
## Common Units
- BTU =  British Thermal Unit
	- energy required to raise one pound of water by 1 degree F
- kWH = Kilowatt hours
	- a watt-hout is the energy one watt of power delivers for one hour
---
## Energy Use in the USA
### Transportation
- ~30% is for transportation
- public transit more efficient, but often less convenient
### Electricity
- ~40% is for generating electricity
- **Increase Efficiency**
	- Power plant
		- *Co-generation* -> use hot water to heat buildings
		- *Combined cycle* -> heat source turns multiple turbines
	- Transmission
		- *Superconduction* -> minimize energy loss
		- *Smart grid* ->smart appliances/meters that respond to local changes in demand/supply, incorporate power storage and renewables

## Non-renewable: Buried Sunlight
- **Petroleum**
	- unconventional oil resources
		- oil shale
		- tar sands/ oil sands (leftover -> petroleum coke)
			1. dig
			2. crush and move
			3. extract
			4. dilute
			5. upgrade
	- liquid and solid hydrocarbons
	- After peak production (the Hubbert Curve):
		- oil shortages
		- price shocks
	- Environmental effects
		- recovery: damage to the ecosystem
		- refining: soil, water and air
		- delivery and use: spills, leaks, air pollution
	- ![[Petroleum.png]]
- **Natural Gas**
	- 80-95% methane
	- used for electricity, industry and Nitrogen Fertilizer
	- Extraction still energy/water intensive and controversial
	- ![[Hydraulic Fracturing.png]]
- **Coal**: Solid fuel formed primarily from remains of trees, ferns, and other plant materials that were preserved 280-360 million years ago.
	- Four types of coal
		- ~~Peat~~
		- Lignite
		- sub-bituminous coal
		- bituminous coal
		- anthracite
	- Mining -> *Mountain top removal*: physcial removal of vegetation and geological material
		- filling of valleys of sediments and removals
		- fragmented/removed forests
		- acidified water
	- ![[Coal.png]]
- **Nuclear**
	- Fission
	- moderator and control rods to create linear nuclear reaction chain
