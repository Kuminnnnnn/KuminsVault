# Fresh water & Marine Resources and pollution
## Types of water pollution
### **Toxic Metal**
1. **Lead** - old lead pipes
	1. Neurotoxic impacts (especially in children)
2. **Mercury** - fossil fuel burning, cement production
3. **Arsenic** - natural in groundwater, wood preservation
	1. lung/kidney/bladder cancer
4. **Selenium** - agricultural runoff
	1. fetus development
### Organics
1. **Pesticides**
2. **PCBs** - electrical equipment, insulation, plastics, etc.
	1. cancer, immune system, endocrine system
3. **Fecal** - human pathogens
	1. hard to determine through all pathogens -> numerous so expensive and difficult
	2. instead, use fecal coliforms
	3. come from sewage or urban runoff
4. *BOD in wastewater*
	1. Biological Oxygen Demand: the amount of oxygen consumed by microorganisms while they decompose organic matter under aerobic conditions at a specified temperature
	2. Low dissolved oxygen -> hypoxia -> death of fish
5. **Algal bloom**
6. **Sediment pollution**
	1. rock and mineral fragments - fills streams, lakes, reservoirs, ponds, harbors
	2. Excess sediments from:
		1. farming
		2. construction
		3. deforestation
		4. off-road vehicles
	3. Chlorine disinfectant commonly used in pulp mills
		1. byproduct = Dioxins (toxic)
	4. PFOAs and PFOs
### **Low pH**
1. Toxic to aquatic life
2. from acid rain, acid mine dranage
### **Thermal discharge**
1. Cause:
	1. volcanic event
	2. power plants
	3. cooling towers
### **Emerging concerns**
1. antibiotics/pharmaceuticals
2. synthetic organic compounds
3. plastics
4. fracking fluid

## Point vs Non-Point Scources
### **Nonpoint(area) Sources** - Diffused and intermittent
- **Stormwater runoff**
	- roads and parking lots
		- oil
		- gasoline
	- farms
		- pesticides
		- fertilizers
	- Forested area
		- soil
		- vegetation
	- Industrial area
		- chemicals
		- minerals
- **Agricultural runoff**
	- primary pollutants
		- nitrates
		- perchlorates
		- hormones
		- pharmaceuticals
	- urine and feces
	- greatest pollution threat
		- Concentrated Animal Feeding Operation (CAFOs)
### **Point Sources** - Distinct and confined
- **Sewage Treatment Plants**
	- >90% of solid and organic waste removed
	- Not all removed
	- discharge poorly mixed
	- Disinfectants toxic to environment
	- foul weather overflow
	- Toxic chemicals and pharmaceuticals not treated: AKA, “emerging contaminants”
- **Power plants and Indsutry**
- **Accidental spills**
	- *Accidental*
		- shipping/boating
		- train/truck
		- industrial/pipeline sites
	- *Most common accidents*
		- spilled petroleum products
		- sewage overflow
		- sewer line breaks
		- oil and gas line breaks
		- flame retardants
- **Oil spills**
	- natural seeps
	- big spills&routine release during shipping
	- urban runoff from roadways
	- *Large spills*
		- Deepwater Horizon
- **Thermal Discharges**
	- Coastal power plants
	- Regulatory loopholes
	- Once-Through Cooling systems (OTC)

## Landmark Federal Water Quality Regulations
need to know what has been done
- **Clean Water Act (1972)**
	- Objective: maintain the chemical, physical, biological integrity of the nation's water
		- fishable and swimmable
	- Priority Pollutants
		- sets regulations and measurement
	- *National pollution discharge elmination system (NPDES)*
		- all point source polluters must have permits
		- places limits on what/how much can be discharged
		- monitoring and reporting
		- translates Clean Water Act into specific provisions tailored to each polluter
	- *Total maximum daily load (TMDL)* -> for impaired water body
- **Safe Drinking Water Act (1974)**
	- Objective: ensure municipal water is safe for human consumption

## Wastewater Treatment
-  **Septic Systems**
	- receives wastewater from a house or small community
		- Sludge: heavier sewage materials that sink to the bottom of the septic tank will be digested
		- Seepage: lighter materials flow out of the tank into a leach fiedl and are filtered into the soil for final digestion
- **Sewage Treatment Plants**
	- Primary - removing solid
	- Secondary - removal of organic material by bacteria
	- Tertiary - removal of nutrients, high purification and disinfection
		- Land application
		- Constructed wetlands
	- *water treatment to human consumption* (需要知道每个的定义)
		- Coagulation and Flocculation  
			- Destabilize particles
			- Form larger flocs
		- Sedimentation
		- Filtration  
		- Disinfection  
		- Storage-> Delivery

## Bio-indicators of Water Quality
Macroinvertebrates as Bioindicators
- Diversity = healthy system
Potential threats to macroinvertebeates
- Sedimentation
- Habitat loss
- Chemical pollution
What makes them good indicators
- spend up to one year in stream
- have little mobility
- generally abundant
- primary food source for many fish
- good indicators of localized conditions
- easy sampling techniques
