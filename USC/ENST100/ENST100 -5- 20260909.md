# Ecosystem Ecology Part1 Biological Production
- **Ecosystem ecology**: study of ecosystem function
- **Biological Production(or Growth)**
	- capture of usable energy from the environment > energy "fixation"
- **Biomass**
	- The total amount of organic matter in an area: (gC/m2) or (MT/ha)
	- Often measured as mass per area
---
## Types of Biological Production
1. **Primary Production (autotrophs)**
	1. make their own organic matter from a source of energy and inorganic compound
	2. photo&chemo
2. **Secondary Production (heterotrophs)**
	1. cannot make own organic compounds
	2. must feed on other living things
---
## Quantifying Production
1. **Gross Primary Production (GPP)**: total amount of CO<sub>2</sub> fixed in an ecosystem
2. **Net Primary Production (NPP)**: energy captured that is converted into biomass
	1. NPP=GPP-respiration
3. **Net Ecosystem Production (NEP)**: Total amount of biomass increase in a system, including heterotrophs and decomposers
	1. NEP=GPP-R<sub>autotrophs</sub>-R<sub>heterotrophs</sub>-R<sub>decomposers</sub>
---
## Food Chain and Energy Flow
- **Food chain**: sequence of transfers of matter and energy in the form of food from primary producer to consumer, to secondary consumer, etc.  
- **Trophic level**: position on food chain
	- affecting food supply (fisheries)
	- affecting movement of toxins
	- affecting carbon storage -> climate implications
- **Trophic efficiency**
	- ***Rule of thumb***: only ~10% of production from one trophic level makes it to the next level above
---
## Biomagnification
- Toxins that cannot be metabolized (and generally are stored in fats) get concentrated up the food chain  
- More trophic levels mean greater toxin concentration
---
# Part2 Biogeochemical cycles
- **Biogeochemical cycles**: The complete path that matter takes through the biotic (biosphere) and abiotic (lithosphere, atmosphere and hydrosphere) compartments of Earth
	- Pools(reservoir)
	- Fluxes
## Limiting Factors
- **Macronutrients**: Elements **required in large quantities by all life** and form the fundamental building blocks of life:
	- carbon
	- oxygen
	- nitrogen
	- potassium
	- calcium
	- phosphorus
	- magnesium
	- sulfur
- **Micronutrients**: Elements required either in small amounts by all life (metals necessary for specific enzymes)
	- Zinc, Iron, B vitamins
