# Genetics and Evolution
- Evolution reflects interaction between **genetics** and **environmental influences**
- **Biodiversity** is driven by evolution

---
- **Evolution**: process by which populations of organisms change over generations
- **Gene**: sequence of nucleotides forming part of a chromosome; the basic physical and functional unit of heredity
- **Allele**: variant forms a gene that arise from mutations
- **Genotype**: the genetic constitution of an organism
- **Genome**: the complete genetic constitution of an organism
---
## Evolution
*A change in the genetic composition of a population overtime*
1. **Microevolution**: the short term adaptations that result from changes in the environment
	1. i.e. changes in allel frequency
	2. Unintentional microevolution e.g. effects of industrial revolution on moth populations in Manchester, England
2. **Macroevolution**: the process of the development of new species from a common ancestor
	1. Intentional Microevolution->Selective breeding, domestication
	2. **Adaptive radiation**:  the diversification of a group of organisms into forms filling different ecological niches
---
- **Part I Descent w/ modification (random process)**
	- New Heritable Traits (gene variants) randomly occur over time, and these traits are passed on to offspring:  
		- Mutation and Recombination  
	- New Gene Variants can be lost via random processes :  
		- Inbreeding
		- Bottleneck Effects
			- only few individuals make it through
		- Founder Effects
			- only few individuals migrate to new area
	- New Gene Variants can be Gained or Lost via:  
		- Genetic Drift (gene variants more commonly lost in small populations)  
		- Migration  
- **Part II. Natural selection (Non-Random Processes)**  
	- If gene mutations are beneficial for survival in a particular environment,  they will be selected for and increase in a population over time.  
	- If they are detrimental, they will be selected against

### How are new species Formed
- **Allopatric Speciation**: physical barrier in place that stops gene flow between populations
	- **Geographic isolation**
		- mountains
		- rivers 
		- volcanoes
		- islands
	-  **Reproductive isolation**
		-   Morphological, physiological or other differences that prevent different species from breeding w/ one another
- **Symatric Speciation**: development of a new species without physical barrier
	- sexual selection
	- plant-pollinator co-evolution
	- environmental change
	- top down pressure by predators or parasites etc.
