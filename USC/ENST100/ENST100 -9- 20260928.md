# Biodiversity
1. **Genetic Diversity**: the amount of gene variability within a given population
	1. Fitness
		1. less likelihood of harmful mutations being expressed
		2. ability to adapt to changing envrironmental conditions
	2. Avoid the extinction vortex
	3. Food Security
2. **Speciese Diversity**: number of different species presents
	1. Species richness: the number of speces in a given area
	2. Species eveness: quantifies how equal the species community is numerically
	3. **Indices**
		1. Simpsons Diversity Index
		2. Shannons Diversity Index
	4. “Species Accumulation curve”: Plots the cumulative observed number of species in a site/environment against the number of samples (collective effort to sample those species)
	5. **Increase diversity**
		1. physically diverse habitat
		2. moderate disturbance
		3. high diversity at a trophic level
		4. habitat highly modified by life
		5. middle stages of succession
		6. evolution
	6. **Decrease diversity**
		1. high environmental stress
		2. extreme environment
		3. severely limited resource
		4. extreme disturbance
		5. recent introduction of an exotic species
		6. geographical isolation (fragmentation)
		7. removal of heystone speces
3. **Functional Diversity**: how many niches occupied
4. **Habitat Diversity**: the range of different species per unit area in an community, ecosystem, or biome
---
## Ecological Succession
- **Primary Succession**: succession on a newly exposed (iceland, volcano) site that was not previously occupied by soil and vegetation
- **Secondary Succession**: occurs on sites that have already supported life but have undergone a disturbance
---
## Species Richness and Species Area Hypothesis
- **Species-Area Hypothesis:** When all else is equal, Larger areas have more resources = greater carrying-capacity = support more species
- **Species-Distance Hypothesis**: the further from source pool, the lower the species diversity
