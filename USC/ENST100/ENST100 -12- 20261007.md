## Shared Resources
- **Public good**
	- *Non-excludable*
		- cannot prevent people who haven't paid for accessing
	- *Non-rivalrous*
		- can be consumed by one without hurting the ability of another to consume
- **Common pool resources**
	- *Difficult to exclude, not impossible*
	- *Divisibility*
		- use by one person subtracts from ability of others to use it
		- Ground water, fisheries, forests, public lands
	- Common pool resources can be managed
		- if not open access
---
## *Tragedy of commons*
- the tendency of a shared, unregulated public resource to become depleted by people acting in self-interest for short-term gain
---
## *Externalities*
- A cost or benefit of a good or service not included in the purchase price of that product or service. Effects person or party that did not participate in the direct transaction or choose the good or service
- tragedy of commons is one **negative externalities**

## Federal Land Management
- **National Parks** – National Park Service (NPS)  
	- Multiple-use principle
		- recreation
		- grazing
		- timber harvesting
		- mineral extraction
	- Threats
		- humans
		- air and water pollution
		- development on edges of park
		- ATVs -> habitat destruction and threaten animals
		- victims of popularity - overuse
- **National Wildlife Refuges** – Fish and Wildlife Services (FWS) 
	- federal public lands managed for primary purpose of protecting wildlife
- **National Forests** – National Forest Service (NFS) 
	- 27% of forests used for logging
	- Selective cutting
- **National Wilderness Areas** – (varies) 
	- preserving large tracts of ecosystems or landscapes
	- allow very limited human activity
		- no logging
		- no roads
		- no mining
- **Rangelands** – Bureau of Land Management (BLM)  
	- large grassland -> grazing

## Residential Land
- **Urban**: metropolitan center
- **Suburban**: lower populaiton-density areas surrounding metropolitan certers
- **Exurban**: similar to suburb but unconnected to any central city or densely populated area
### Urban Sprawl
- Induced demand feedback loop
	- suburbs expand ->
	- increased traffic congestion ->
	- longer commutes ->
	- more gas used ->
	- increased gasoline tax revenues ->
	- more highway construction ->
	- suburbs expand
- Four main causes:
	1. automobiles and highway construction
	2. lower living costs -> larger houses
	3. government policies
	4. **urban blight**: Feedback loop - city revenue shrinks as people move to the suburbs -> less money in city -> decreased city
### Smart growth
- residential land use solution: focuses on strategies to encourage development of sustainable, healthy commuties
- **10 Pinciples:**
	1. Create *Mixed land uses*
	2. Offer a *range of housing* opportunities and choices
	3. Create *walkable neighborhoods*
	4. Encourage *community and stakeholder collaboration* in development decisions
	5. Take advantage of *compact building*
	6. Foster distinctive, attractive communities with a *strong sense of place*
	7. *Preserve open space*, farmland, natural beauty, and critical environmental areas
	8. Provide a *variety of transportation* choices
		- Transit-oriented development (TOD)
	9. Strengthen and direct development toward *existing communites*
		- Infill development
		- Urban growth boundaries
	10. Make development decisions *predictable, fair, and cost-effective*
