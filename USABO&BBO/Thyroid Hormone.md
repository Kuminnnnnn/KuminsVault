[[Human Hormones]]
### **Synthesis of Thyroid Hormones**

The synthesis of thyroid hormones (T₃ and T₄) is a multi-step process dependent on iodine, tyrosine, and thyroglobulin. Below is a detailed breakdown:

---

#### ​**1. Iodide Uptake and Activation**

- ​**Steps**:
    1. ​**Iodide Uptake**: Thyroid follicular cells actively transport iodide (I⁻) from the bloodstream via the ​**sodium-iodide symporter (NIS)**.
    2. ​**Iodide Oxidation**: I⁻ is oxidized to ​**reactive iodine (I⁰)** by the enzyme ​**thyroid peroxidase (TPO)**.
- ​**Key Enzyme**: Thyroid peroxidase (TPO).

---

#### ​**2. Iodination of Tyrosine**

- ​**Steps**:
    1. ​**Thyroglobulin (Tg)**: A glycoprotein synthesized by follicular cells, containing multiple tyrosine residues.
    2. ​**Iodination**: Reactive iodine (I⁰) binds to tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin, forming:
        - ​**Monoiodotyrosine (MIT)** (tyrosine + 1 iodine atom)
        - ​**Diiodotyrosine (DIT)** (tyrosine + 2 iodine atoms).
- ​**Reaction**:  
    ​**Tyrosine + I⁰ → MIT → DIT**

---

#### ​**3. Coupling Reaction**

- ​**Steps**:
    1. ​**T₄ Synthesis**: One DIT couples with another DIT → ​**thyroxine (T₄, tetraiodothyronine)**.
    2. ​**T₃ Synthesis**: One MIT couples with one DIT → ​**triiodothyronine (T₃)**.
- ​**Key Enzyme**: TPO catalyzes the coupling reaction.

---

#### ​**4. Storage and Release**

- ​**Storage**: Newly synthesized T₃ and T₄ remain bound to thyroglobulin, stored in the ​**colloid** within the follicular lumen.
- ​**Release**:
    1. Upon thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) stimulation, follicular cells engulf colloid via endocytosis.
    2. ​**Lysosomes** hydrolyze thyroglobulin, releasing free T₃ and T₄ into the bloodstream.

---

#### ​**5. Regulation**

- ​**Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)**:
    - TSH (from the pituitary) binds to receptors on thyroid cells → activates cAMP → enhances iodide uptake, thyroglobulin synthesis, and hormone release.
- ​**Negative Feedback**:
    - Elevated blood T₃/T₄ levels suppress TSH secretion via the ​**hypothalamus-pituitary-thyroid axis**.

---

#### ​**6. Clinical Relevance**

- ​**Hyperthyroidism**: Excessive T₃/T₄ synthesis (e.g., Graves' disease).
- ​**Hypothyroidism**: Deficient synthesis (e.g., iodine deficiency, TPO defects).
- ​**Drug Targets**: Antithyroid drugs (e.g., methimazole) inhibit TPO, blocking iodination and coupling.
