### **Major Fungal Phyla: Overview and Characteristics**

The **common ancestor** of fungi was an **aquatic, single-celled, flagellated protist**

Fungi are classified into several phyla based on their reproductive structures, genetic makeup, and ecological roles. Below is an overview of the key fungal phyla, their defining features, and examples:
![[Fungi phylum.png]]
---

#### ​**1. Ascomycota (Sac Fungi)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Produce sexual spores (ascospores) within sac-like structures called ​**asci** (singular: ascus).
    - Asexual reproduction via ​**conidia** (spores formed on specialized hyphae).
    - Includes unicellular yeasts (e.g., _Saccharomyces_) and multicellular species.
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Decomposers, plant pathogens (e.g., Dutch elm disease), and mutualists (e.g., lichens).
- ​**Examples**:
    - Morels (_Morchella_), truffles (_Tuber_), penicillin-producing _Penicillium_, and _Aspergillus_.

---

#### ​**2. Basidiomycota (Club Fungi)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Sexual spores (basidiospores) form on club-shaped structures called ​**basidia**.
    - Often produce large fruiting bodies (e.g., mushrooms, puffballs).
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Decomposers of wood (e.g., _Agaricus_ mushrooms), plant pathogens (e.g., rusts and smuts), and mycorrhizal partners.
- ​**Examples**:
    - Button mushrooms (_Agaricus bisporus_), shiitake (_Lentinula edodes_), and the pathogenic wheat rust (_Puccinia graminis_).

---

#### ​**3. Mucoromycota (Formerly Zygomycota)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Sexual reproduction via ​**zygospores** (thick-walled spores formed by fusion of hyphae).
    - Asexual reproduction via sporangia producing ​**sporangiospores**.
    - Mostly terrestrial saprobes or plant symbionts.
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Decomposers of organic matter; some form endomycorrhizae.
- ​**Examples**:
    - Bread mold (_Rhizopus stolonifer_), _Mucor_, and _Glomus_ (mycorrhizal fungi).

---

#### ​**4. Chytridiomycota (Chytrids)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Produce motile spores (**zoospores**) with a single flagellum.
    - Simplest and most primitive fungi, often aquatic or soil-dwelling.
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Decomposers of cellulose/chitin; some parasitize plants, animals (e.g., amphibians).
- ​**Examples**:
    - _Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis_ (causes chytridiomycosis in frogs), _Allomyces_.

---

#### ​**5. Glomeromycota (Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Obligate symbionts forming ​**arbuscular mycorrhizae** with plant roots.
    - No known sexual reproduction; reproduce asexually via ​**glomerospores**.
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Critical for nutrient exchange (especially phosphorus) in >80% of land plants.
- ​**Examples**:
    - _Rhizophagus irregularis_ (formerly _Glomus intraradices_).

---

#### ​**6. Microsporidia (Controversial Group)**

- ​**Key Features**:
    - Unicellular, spore-forming parasites with highly reduced genomes.
    - Infect animals (including humans) via polar tubes that inject spores into host cells.
- ​**Ecological Roles**:
    - Obligate intracellular parasites; some cause disease in immunocompromised hosts.
- ​**Examples**:
    - _Encephalitozoon cuniculi_ (infects mammals), _Nosema apis_ (parasitizes honeybees).

---
