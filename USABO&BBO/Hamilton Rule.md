### **Hamilton's Rule Explained**

**Hamilton's rule** is a foundational concept in evolutionary biology that predicts when ​**altruistic behaviors** (actions that reduce an individual’s fitness to benefit others) can evolve through ​**kin selection**. It quantifies the conditions under which natural selection favors altruism toward relatives.

---

#### ​**1. The Formula**

> rB>C

- ​**r**: ​**Coefficient of relatedness** (genetic similarity between the altruist and recipient).
    - Represents the probability that two individuals share identical copies of a gene due to common ancestry.
- ​**B**: ​**Benefit** to the recipient’s reproductive success (increase in offspring survival/reproduction).
- ​**C**: ​**Cost** to the altruist’s reproductive success (decrease in offspring survival/reproduction).

**Interpretation**: Altruism evolves if the genetic benefit to the recipient (rB) exceeds the cost to the altruist (C).

---

#### ​**2. Key Concepts**

1. ​**Inclusive Fitness**:
    
    - An individual’s total evolutionary success includes:
        - ​**Direct fitness**: Personal offspring production.
        - ​**Indirect fitness**: Additional offspring equivalents produced by aiding relatives.
    - Hamilton’s rule focuses on maximizing ​**inclusive fitness**.
2. ​**Kin Selection**:
    
    - Natural selection favors altruism when it enhances the survival/reproduction of genetically related individuals.
    - Explains behaviors like warning calls, cooperative breeding, and self-sacrifice in social animals.

---

#### ​**3. Calculating r (Relatedness)**

|​**Relationship**|​**Coefficient (r)**|
|---|---|
|Parent-Child or Full Siblings|0.5|
|Half-Siblings|0.25|
|Grandparent-Grandchild|0.25|
|Aunt/Uncle-Niece/Nephew|0.25|
|Cousins|0.125|
|Unrelated Individuals|0|

---

#### ​**4. Example**

**Scenario**: A ground squirrel emits an alarm call to warn relatives of a predator.

- ​**Cost (C)**: 0.5 (reduced survival chance for the caller).
- ​**Benefit (B)**: 1.5 (increased survival chance for each relative).
- ​**Threshold**: BC​=1.50.5​≈0.333.

**Application**:

- For full siblings (r=0.5):rB=0.5×1.5=0.75>C=0.5→ Altruism evolves.
- For cousins (r=0.125):rB=0.125×1.5=0.1875<C=0.5→ Altruism does not evolve.

---

#### ​**5. When Does Hamilton’s Rule Apply?**

- ​**Recipients must be relatives**: Higher r increases the likelihood of altruism.
- ​**Fitness trade-offs**: The benefit to relatives must outweigh the cost to the altruist.
- ​**Behavior must be heritable**: The altruistic trait must have a genetic basis.

---

#### ​**6. Significance**

- Explains altruism in social insects (e.g., worker bees sacrificing reproduction to aid the queen).
- Predicts cooperation in family groups (e.g., meerkats sharing guard duties).
- Highlights the role of genetic relatedness in social evolution.
