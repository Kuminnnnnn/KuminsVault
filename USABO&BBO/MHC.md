### **Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC): Structure, Function, and Biological Significance**

**Definition**:  
The ​**Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC)** is a group of cell surface proteins essential for the adaptive immune system. These molecules present antigenic peptides to T cells, enabling immune recognition and response. MHC proteins are encoded by highly polymorphic genes and play a central role in ​**self/non-self discrimination** and ​**transplant rejection**.

---

#### ​**1. Classification of MHC Molecules**

|​**Class**|​**Structure**|​**Expression**|​**Function**|
|---|---|---|---|
|​**MHC Class I**|α chain + β2-microglobulin (non-covalently bound)|All nucleated cells|Present ​**endogenous antigens** (e.g., viral proteins) to ​**CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells**.|
|​**MHC Class II**|α chain + β chain (both transmembrane)|Antigen-presenting cells (APCs): dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells|Present ​**exogenous antigens** (e.g., bacterial proteins) to ​**CD4⁺ helper T cells**.|

---

#### ​**2. Antigen Presentation Pathways**

1. ​**MHC Class I Pathway (Endogenous Pathway)**:
    
    - ​**Source of antigens**: Intracellular pathogens (e.g., viruses), tumor antigens.
    - ​**Process**:
        - Proteasomes degrade proteins into peptides.
        - Peptides are transported to the ER via ​**TAP (Transporter Associated with Antigen Processing)**.
        - Peptides bind to MHC I in the ER → MHC I-peptide complex is transported to the cell surface.
    - ​**T cell activation**: CD8⁺ T cells recognize MHC I-peptide complexes and kill infected cells.
2. ​**MHC Class II Pathway (Exogenous Pathway)**:
    
    - ​**Source of antigens**: Extracellular pathogens (e.g., bacteria, parasites).
    - ​**Process**:
        - APCs internalize antigens via phagocytosis or endocytosis.
        - Antigens are degraded in lysosomes.
        - MHC II molecules bind peptides in endosomes → MHC II-peptide complex is displayed on the cell surface.
    - ​**T cell activation**: CD4⁺ T cells recognize MHC II-peptide complexes and orchestrate immune responses (e.g., cytokine release).
3. ​**Cross-Presentation (Unique to Dendritic Cells)**:
    
    - ​**Mechanism**: Exogenous antigens are presented via ​**MHC Class I** to activate CD8⁺ T cells.
    - ​**Significance**: Critical for antiviral and antitumor immunity.

---

#### ​**3. MHC Polymorphism and Genetic Diversity**

- ​**Polygeny**: Multiple MHC genes exist (e.g., HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C in humans).
- ​**Polymorphism**: Each MHC gene has hundreds of alleles within a population.
- ​**Biological Impact**:
    - Ensures diverse antigen presentation, enhancing population-level resistance to pathogens.
    - Complicates organ transplantation (requires HLA matching).

---

#### ​**4. MHC and Disease**

1. ​**Autoimmune Diseases**:
    
    - ​**Example**: Type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis.
    - ​**Mechanism**: Certain MHC alleles (e.g., HLA-DR4) present self-antigens, triggering autoimmunity.
2. ​**Infectious Diseases**:
    
    - ​**Example**: HIV, hepatitis B.
    - ​**Mechanism**: Pathogens evade immunity by mutating MHC-binding epitopes.
3. ​**Transplant Rejection**:
    
    - ​**Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD)**: Donor T cells attack recipient tissues due to MHC mismatch.****
