## **Definition**
>Phototropism is the directional growth of a plant organ (e.g., stems, leaves) in response to ​**unilateral light**, causing the organism to bend toward (_positive phototropism_) or away from (_negative phototropism_) the light source.

---
## ​**Key Mechanisms**:

1. ​**Light Perception**:
    - Mediated by ​**phototropins** (blue-light receptors, e.g., PHOT1 and PHOT2) in plant cells.
    - Light gradients are detected at the organ tip (e.g., shoot apex).

2. ​**Hormonal Regulation**: [[Plants Hormones]]
    - Asymmetric ​**auxin redistribution** occurs:
        - Light side: ***Auxin*** efflux carriers (e.g., PIN3 proteins) pump auxin away.
        - Shaded side: ***Auxin*** accumulates, stimulating cell elongation via acid growth hypothesis.

3. ​**Growth Response**:
    >	**Cells on the shaded side elongate more, causing bending toward the light.**

---
## ​**Examples**:
- ​**Positive phototropism**: Sunflower stems tracking sunlight (_heliotropism_), houseplants leaning toward windows.
- ​**Negative phototropism**: Roots growing away from light (e.g., _Arabidopsis_ roots in soil).
