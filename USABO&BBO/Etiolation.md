> 黄化现象

**Etiolation** is an adaptive morphological change in plants growing in ​**continuous darkness**, characterized by ​**abnormal elongation, hindered leaf development, and chlorophyll deficiency**. This phenomenon represents a survival strategy where plants adjust resource allocation to ​**prioritize finding light sources**, involving a complex light signal regulation network.

---

### ​**Core Features**:

1. ​**Morphological Changes**:
    - ​**Excessive Stem Elongation**: Accelerated longitudinal expansion of internode cells (3–5 times faster than normal growth).
    - ​**Yellowing and Curling of Leaves**: Cotyledons or true leaves fail to expand, reducing leaf area by >70%.
    - ​**Retention of Apical Hook**: The stem apex maintains a hooked morphology in darkness (protecting meristematic tissues).

2. ​**Physiological Changes**:
    - ​**Chloroplast Degeneration into Etioplasts**: Lack thylakoid membrane structures and accumulate protochlorophyllide.
    - ​**Thinning of Cell Walls**: Reduced cellulose content and hydroxyproline-rich proteins.
    - ​**Depletion of Starch Reserves**: Energy is prioritized for stem elongation over storage.

---

### ​**Molecular Regulatory Mechanisms**:

1. ​**Phytochrome Dominance**:
    - In darkness, phytochromes exist in the ​**Pr form**, unable to activate downstream genes.
    - Red light (660 nm) triggers ​**Pr→Pfr conversion**, initiating de-etiolation.

2. ​**Hormone Regulation Network**: [[Plants Hormones]]
    - ​**Auxin**: Enhances polar transport to promote cell elongation (via cell wall acidification and activation of expansins).
    - ​**Gibberellins (GA)**: Interact with DELLA proteins to relieve inhibition on PIFs.
    - ​**Ethylene**: Suppresses etiolation by antagonizing PIFs through the EIN3/EIL1 pathway under light.

3. ​**Transcription Factors PIFs (Phytochrome-Interacting Factors)**:
    - PIFs accumulate in darkness, activating ​**YUC genes** (promoting auxin biosynthesis) and ​**EXPANSIN genes** (cell wall loosening).
    - Light signals degrade PIFs via phyA/phyB, terminating the etiolation program.
