## **Key Components**
1. **Pith**
	- Center of the woody stem
	- Stores water and starch
2. **Vascular cambium**
	- Secondary growth tissue
	- where xylem and phloem grow
3. **Vascular ray**
	- Provide structural support
	- Horizontal transportation
	- Store water and starch

## **Secondary Growth**
### ​**1. Definition of Secondary Growth**

- ​**Core Concept**: Thickening growth produced by ​**lateral meristems** (vascular cambium and cork cambium), increasing the diameter of stems and roots.
- ​**Occurrence**:
    - Common in ​**gymnosperms** and most ​**dicots** (e.g., trees, shrubs).
    - Rare in ​**monocots** (exceptions like bamboo).

---

### ​**2. Dynamic Process of Secondary Growth**

#### ​**Stage 1: Completion of Primary Growth** 

- Apical meristem activity ceases; vascular cambium forms between primary xylem and phloem.

#### ​**Stage 2: Development of Secondary Vascular Tissues** 

- ​**Secondary xylem accumulation**:
    - ​**Earlywood (spring wood)**: Large, thin-walled cells forming lighter-colored, loose layers.
    - ​**Latewood (summer wood)**: Small, thick-walled cells forming darker, dense layers.
    - ​**Annual rings**: One concentric ring composed of earlywood + latewood per year.
- ​**Secondary phloem compression**:
    - Secondary xylem growth inward pushes secondary phloem outward, causing rupture and shedding of outer primary phloem and cortex.

#### ​**Stage 3: Periderm Replaces Epidermis** (Step 4 in Figure 35.19)

- ​**Epidermal rupture**: Periderm (cork layer) replaces the epidermis as stem thickening progresses.
- ​**Lenticels**: Gas exchange structures on the periderm (e.g., oxygen entry into internal living cells).
