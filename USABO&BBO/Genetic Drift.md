### **Genetic Drift: Definition, Mechanisms, and Examples**

**Genetic drift** is a mechanism of evolution that causes ​**random changes in allele frequencies** within a population due to chance events. Unlike natural selection, which favors traits that improve survival or reproduction, genetic drift is entirely ​**non-adaptive** and occurs more strongly in small populations.

---

#### ​**1. Key Characteristics**

- ​**Random Process**: Changes in allele frequencies are driven by luck, not fitness.
- ​**Population Size Dependent**: Effects are magnified in small populations.
- ​**Reduces Genetic Diversity**: Can lead to the loss of alleles (including beneficial ones) or fixation of alleles (frequency reaches 100%).

---

#### ​**2. Mechanisms of Genetic Drift**

1. ​**Bottleneck Effect**:
    - A sudden reduction in population size (e.g., natural disasters, disease) leaves a subset of survivors whose gene pool may not represent the original population.
    - ​**Example**: Cheetahs underwent a severe bottleneck ~10,000 years ago, leading to extremely low genetic diversity.

2. ​**Founder Effect**:
    - A small group of individuals establishes a new population, carrying only a fraction of the original gene pool.
    - ​**Example**: The Amish community in Pennsylvania has a high frequency of Ellis-van Creveld syndrome due to founder effect.

---

#### ​**3. Mathematical Representation**

- ​**Allele Frequency Variance**:  
    The magnitude of genetic drift is inversely proportional to population size (N):Variance in allele frequency∝2N1​
- ​**Fixation Probability**:  
    In a diploid population of size N, the probability that a neutral allele becomes fixed is 2N1​.

#### ​**4. Examples of Genetic Drift**

|​**Scenario**|​**Outcome**|
|---|---|
|​**Coin Toss Analogy**|Imagine flipping a coin 10 times (small population) vs. 1,000 times (large population). Small samples are more likely to deviate from 50% heads/tails.|
|​**Island Colonization**|A few birds blown to an isolated island may carry rare alleles that dominate the new population.|
|​**Laboratory Fruit Fly Populations**|Small lab populations often show random allele fixation/loss over generations.|
