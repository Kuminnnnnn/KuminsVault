#### **1. Natural Killer Cells (NK Cells)**

**Function**:

- ​**Innate Immunity**: NK cells are part of the innate immune system and provide rapid, ***nonspecific*** defense against infected or cancerous cells.
- ​**Targets**: Virus-infected cells, tumor cells, and stressed cells (e.g., lacking MHC I molecules).

**Recognition Mechanisms**:

- ​"Missing Self" Hypothesis: Detect cells with reduced or absent ​**MHC I molecules** (common in cancer/viral infection).[[MHC]]
- ​**Activating Receptors**:
    - ​**NKG2D**: Binds stress-induced ligands (e.g., MICA/MICB on stressed cells).
    - ​**NCRs (Natural Cytotoxicity Receptors)**: Recognize viral/bacterial proteins.
- ​**Inhibitory Receptors**:
    - ​**KIRs (Killer Immunoglobulin-like Receptors)** and ​**CD94/NKG2A**: Bind MHC I molecules to prevent killing of healthy cells.

**Activation & Effector Mechanisms**:

1. ​**Direct Killing**:
    - Release ​**perforin** (forms pores in target cell membranes) and ​**granzymes** (induce apoptosis).
    - ​**Fas/FasL Pathway**: Engage Fas on target cells to trigger apoptosis.
2. ​**Cytokine Production**: Secrete ​**IFN-γ** to enhance macrophage activation and adaptive immunity.

**Key Features**:

- ​**No prior sensitization required**: Act immediately without antigen presentation.
- ​**No immunological memory**: Function independently of adaptive immune memory.

#### ​**2. Cytotoxic T Cells (CTLs)**

**Function**:
[[MHC]]
- ​**Adaptive Immunity**: CTLs are antigen-specific and provide long-term, targeted immunity.
- ​**Targets**: Cells presenting ​**MHC I-bound antigens** (e.g., virus-infected cells, intracellular pathogens). -> **Require the help from TCR**

**Recognition Mechanisms**:

- ​**T Cell Receptor (TCR)**: Binds ​**MHC I-peptide complexes** on target cells.
- ​**Co-stimulation**: Requires ​**CD8** (binds MHC I) and ​**CD28-B7 interaction** for full activation.

**Activation & Effector Mechanisms**:

1. ​**Activation Steps**:
    - ​**Priming**: Naïve CD8⁺ T cells are activated by dendritic cells presenting antigen via MHC I.
    - ​**Clonal Expansion**: Differentiate into effector CTLs and memory T cells.
2. ​**Direct Killing**:
    - Use ​**perforin** and ​**granzymes** (same as NK cells) to induce apoptosis.
    - ​**Fas/FasL Pathway**: Enhances apoptosis in target cells.
3. ​**Cytokine Release**: Secrete ​**IFN-γ** and ​**TNF-α** to amplify immune responses.

**Key Features**:

- ​**Antigen-specific**: Recognize unique peptides presented by MHC I.
- ​**Immunological Memory**: Generate memory CTLs for faster response upon re-infection.

### ​**Comparison Table**

|​**Feature**|​**NK Cells**|​**Cytotoxic T Cells (CTLs)**|
|---|---|---|
|​**Immune System**|Innate|Adaptive|
|​**Antigen Specificity**|Nonspecific|Highly specific (MHC I-restricted)|
|​**Activation Trigger**|Missing MHC I, stress ligands|MHC I + antigen peptide + co-stimulation|
|​**Memory Formation**|No|Yes (memory CTLs)|
|​**Key Receptors**|NKG2D, KIRs, NCRs|TCR, CD8, CD28|
|​**Effector Molecules**|Perforin, granzymes, FasL|Perforin, granzymes, FasL|
|​**Role in Cancer**|First-line defense, kill MHC I-low tumors|Eliminate antigen-expressing cancer cells|

---

### ​**Biological Significance**

1. ​**NK Cells**:
    - Critical for controlling early viral infections and tumors.
    - Participate in ​**antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)** by binding IgG via CD16 (FcγRIII).
2. ​**CTLs**:
    - Essential for clearing intracellular pathogens (e.g., HIV, _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_).
    - Central to ​**vaccine efficacy** (e.g., COVID-19 vaccines prime CTL responses).
