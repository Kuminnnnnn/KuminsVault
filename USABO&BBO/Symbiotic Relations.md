 ### ​**Comparison of Symbiotic Relationships**

|​**Type**|​**Definition**|​**Example**|​**Key Features**|
|---|---|---|---|
|​**Commensalism**|One species benefits; the other is ​**unaffected**.|- Basket fern (_Aglaomorpha_) grows on trees for support (no harm to the tree).  <br>- Barnacles on whales.|Neutral for one partner, beneficial for the other. Common in epiphytes or scavengers.|
|​**Mutualism**|​**Both species benefit** from the interaction.|- Bees pollinate flowers (bees get nectar; plants reproduce).  <br>- Clownfish and sea anemones.|Mutual dependence; often evolutionary co-adaptation. Critical for ecosystem stability.|
|​**Parasitism**|One species (**parasite**) benefits; the other (**host**) is ​**harmed**.|- Dodder (_Cuscuta_) steals nutrients from host plants.  <br>- Tapeworms in mammals.|Host suffers reduced fitness; parasites often highly specialized.|
|​**Amensalism**|One species is ​**harmed**; the other is ​**unaffected**.|- Black walnut trees release juglone, inhibiting nearby plant growth.  <br>- Antibiotic-producing fungi killing bacteria.|Asymmetric harm; often via chemical or physical interference.|

---

### ​**Benefit/Harm Balance**:
- ​**Mutualism**: +/+ (both gain).
- ​**Commensalism**: +/0 (one gains, one neutral).
- ​**Parasitism**: +/- (one gains, one loses).
- ​**Amensalism**: -/0 (one loses, one neutral).
