
## Core logic
>Ligand → Receptor → Kinase cascade → Target protein phosphorylation → Functional changes
![[Cascade.jpg]]
---
### Ligand
- Signaling molecule
---
### Receptor
- Produce relay molecule when bind
- Relay molecule: activates protein kinase
- Kinase: casts for **phosphorylation**
---
### Functional Change - **Nuclear Translocation**
- Nuclear Translocation: protein move from cytoplasm to nuclear
#### Methods
1. Phosphorylation - change its shape or activity
2. Phosphomimetic Mutation - switch amino acids to mimic phosphorylation
---
## Examples - [[Epinephrine]] → Glycogen Breakdown
![[Signal Transduction.jpg]]

1. ​**Epinephrine binds to β-adrenergic receptors** (a type of cell membrane receptor).
2. ​**Activates G proteins** → Stimulates ​**adenylate cyclase** to produce ​**cAMP** (a second messenger).
3. ​**cAMP activates protein kinase A (PKA)**.
4. ​**PKA phosphorylates phosphorylase kinase** → Activates ​**glycogen phosphorylase**.
5. ​**Glycogen is broken down into glucose**, providing energy for the cell.

---

### ​**Key Terms Explained**

- ​**β-adrenergic receptor**: A G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) on the cell membrane.
- ​**Adenylate cyclase**: Enzyme that converts ATP to cAMP. ^c07d9c
- ​**cAMP**: Cyclic adenosine monophosphate, a secondary messenger amplifying the signal.
	- Made from ATP by Adenylyl Cyclase [[#^c07d9c]]
	- Inactivated by Phosphodiesterase
	- Amplify the signal
	![[cAMP.jpg]]
- ​**Protein kinase A (PKA)**: Activated by cAMP, triggers phosphorylation cascades.
- ​**Phosphorylase kinase/glycogen phosphorylase**: Enzymes driving glycogen breakdown.
