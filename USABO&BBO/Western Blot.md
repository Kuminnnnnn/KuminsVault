**Western Blot** (also known as ​**protein immunoblotting**) is a widely used technique in molecular biology, biochemistry, and medical research to detect the presence and molecular weight of specific proteins in complex mixtures (e.g., cell lysates). Its core principle relies on the ​**specific binding of antibodies to target proteins**, followed by visualization using chemiluminescent or fluorescent signals.

---

### ​**Experimental Workflow Overview**

1. ​**Sample Preparation**
    
    - Lyse cells or tissues to extract total proteins.
    - Add ​**SDS (sodium dodecyl sulfate)** and ​**reducing agents (e.g., β-mercaptoethanol)**:
        - ​**SDS** denatures proteins and imparts a uniform negative charge.
        - ​**Reducing agents** break disulfide bonds, separating subunits of multi-subunit proteins.
    - Heat samples (95°C, 5 minutes) to fully denature proteins.
2. ​**SDS-PAGE Electrophoresis**
    
    - Load samples onto a ​**polyacrylamide gel** to separate proteins under an electric field.
    - ​**Separation principle**: Smaller proteins migrate faster, while larger proteins migrate slower.
    - A ​**molecular weight marker** is used to determine the migration position of target proteins.
3. ​**Transfer (Blotting)**
    
    - Transfer proteins from the gel to a ​**solid-phase membrane** (commonly nitrocellulose or PVDF membrane).
    - ​**Transfer methods**:
        - ​**Wet transfer** (traditional, suitable for most molecular weights).
        - ​**Semi-dry transfer** (faster, ideal for small proteins).
    - Verify transfer efficiency (e.g., by staining residual proteins in the gel with Coomassie Blue).
4. ​**Blocking**
    
    - Use ​**blocking agents** (e.g., non-fat milk or BSA) to cover non-specific binding sites on the membrane, preventing nonspecific antibody adsorption.
    - Blocking duration: 1 hour to overnight (adjust based on antibody specificity).
5. ​**Antibody Incubation**
    
    - ​**Primary antibody incubation**:
        - Use a target-specific primary antibody, incubating at room temperature for 1 hour or at 4°C overnight.
        - Optimize antibody dilution (e.g., 1:1000–1:5000).
    - ​**Secondary antibody incubation**:
        - Use a species-matched enzyme-conjugated secondary antibody (e.g., HRP-labeled anti-rabbit IgG).
        - The secondary antibody amplifies the signal; typically incubated for 1 hour.
6. ​**Signal Detection**
    
    - ​**Chemiluminescence** (most common):
        - Add substrate (e.g., ECL), where HRP catalyzes light emission, captured by X-ray film or imaging systems.
    - ​**Fluorescence**:
        - Use fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for direct detection via imaging systems.
