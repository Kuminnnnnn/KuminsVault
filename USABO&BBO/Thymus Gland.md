>胸腺

## **Core Functions**

1. ​**T Cell Differentiation and Maturation**:
    
    - The thymus acts as a "training camp" for T lymphocytes (T cells). Immature T cells (precursor T cells) produced in the bone marrow migrate to the thymus, where they undergo ​**positive selection** (recognizing self-MHC molecules) and ​**negative selection** (eliminating cells that attack self-tissues). These processes ultimately differentiate them into functional T cells (e.g., helper T cells, cytotoxic T cells).
    - T cells that fail selection (approximately 95%) are cleared via apoptosis to prevent autoimmune reactions.
2. ​**Endocrine Function**:
    
    - Secretes ​**thymic hormones** (e.g., thymosin, thymopoietin), which promote T cell maturation and maintain immune system functionality. [[Human Hormones]]
### **Clinical Relevance**

1. ​**Immunodeficiency**:
    
    - ​**Congenital Thymic Hypoplasia (DiGeorge Syndrome)**: Caused by a deletion in chromosome 22, leading to thymic absence or maldevelopment. **Patients lack T cells** and are prone to infections.
    - ​**Myasthenia Gravis**: Some patients have thymomas or thymic hyperplasia; thymectomy may alleviate symptoms.
### **Connections to Other Organs**

- ​**Bone Marrow**: Supplies T cell precursors (bone marrow → thymus → mature T cells).
- ​**Lymph Nodes/Spleen**: Mature T cells migrate to peripheral immune organs to perform their functions.
