## **Phytochrome**
**Phytochrome** is a central photoreceptor protein in plants that detects ​**red light (660 nm)** and ​**far-red light (730 nm)**, regulating photomorphogenesis, photoperiodism, and circadian rhythms.

- ​**Two Interconvertible Forms**:
    - ​**Pr (Red light-absorbing form)**: Absorbs at 660 nm, inactive (stable in darkness or far-red light).
    - ​**Pfr (Far-red light-absorbing form)**: Absorbs at 730 nm, biologically active (converted from Pr by red light).
>![[Pr Pfr Interconversion.png]]

#### **Key Mechanisms**:
1. ​**Phytochrome Structure**:
    - ​**Apoprotein**: Encoded by the _PHY_ gene family, providing the protein backbone.
    - ​**Chromophore**: A linear tetrapyrrole (phytochromobilin) covalently bound to the apoprotein, responsible for absorbing red (660 nm) and far-red light (730 nm).
2. ​**Functional Dependency**:
    - The chromophore is essential for ​**Pr↔Pfr photoconversion**. Its removal disables phytochrome’s light-sensing ability.
    - Loss of photoperiod detection→Failure to synthesize/transport florigen (e.g., FT protein).[[Photoperiodism]]
